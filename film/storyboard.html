In the Priesthood of the Sun

Rhodes, 255 BC. One ordinary day in the life of a nineteen-year-old jar-maker, lived under a bronze god he never quite looks at.

The Colossus is context, never subject. We see its light, its shadow, its face on stamps and coins, its toes at night. Never the whole statue. Never a hero shot.

Look, sound and the rules

Cinematic still, 2.39:1 feel inside a 16:9 frame, shot on 65mm film, natural and practical light only, soft film grain, restrained colour: terracotta, raw clay, undyed wool, sea blue, olive green, bronze gold only where the Sun's light falls. Painterly realism in the spirit of Roger Deakins and Terrence Malick. Human scale, lived-in textures, dust, sweat, soot.

  • M1 from S01, 95 s. Solo ancient Greek kithara (lyre), plucked slowly, sparse and tender, a single aulos drone underneath, dawn, intimate, no percussion, no modern instruments
  • M2 from S19, 70 s. Warm rhythmic hand frame-drum and kithara ostinato, heat of afternoon, youthful and bright, ancient Mediterranean, no modern instruments
  • M3 from S26, 110 s. Night. A single low aulos breathing long notes over a soft kithara, grief and wonder together, builds very slightly then stops dead in silence, ancient Greek, no percussion

Dialogue is staged so that lips rarely carry the line: backs, profiles, hands, wide frames and off-screen voices. It suits the film's reticence, and it keeps generated faces from having to lip-sync.

The Colossus appears only as light on faces, a cropped bronze shin at dawn, the face on a stamp and a coin, and its toes above a pedestal at night. It is never shown whole.
