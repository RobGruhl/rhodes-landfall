# A birthday in Rhodes: nine hours, one adult, the Italian Dream of the East

## The frame
Scarlet Lady ties up on Akti Sachtouri, the quay of the "Tourist Port" on the east flank of the walled city. From the gangway the Marine Gate and the Gate of the Virgin are a five-minute stroll; the harbour front and Mandraki are ten. Taxis wait at the terminal and at Rimini Square by the Nea Agora. Rhodes taxis are metered (flag fall €3.72 including the first 2.7 km, then €0.90/km by day; +€1.07 port surcharge), but drivers quote fixed prices for the standard runs: roughly €15-20 to Kallithea Springs (15 min), €20-22 to Anthony Quinn Bay (20 min). Agree the fare before you get in. Sunset is 18:52, after all-aboard, so this is a daylight birthday: build it around water, marble and cake.

## Option A - the Royal Thermae (Kallithea Springs)
Nine kilometres down the east coast, a cove of reddish rock where "reddish water surging from a particular, rocky formation" drew the sick for centuries; the Ottoman-era locals called it *Tsilonero* and swore by it for arthritis, skin and asthma. Governor Mario Lago commissioned a scientific study in 1927, then in 1928 handed the site to architect Pietro Lombardi with Armando Bernabiti on decoration. The result was inaugurated on 1 July 1929 as the *Regie Terme di Calitea* - Art Deco with Moorish teeth: a small rotunda "with six gates in Moorish style" around a statue of Eros, six fountains feeding a central pool; then a larger rotunda whose elliptical arcade of benches looks over the bay, its dome "supported by two semi-domes"; every path paved in black-and-white pebble mosaics of plants and geometry, outer walls painted with sea creatures. The 1930s brought an international hydrology congress of "more than 200 scientific specialists" and a jet-set clientele.

Then the war. The Germans "turned the complex into a penitentiary. Barbed wire and minefields had transformed the once impressive spa into a place of dismay." Partial reopenings on Marshall Aid money, then closure in 1967. Hollywood found the ruin first: *The Guns of Navarone* (1961) with Peck, Niven and Quinn; *Escape to Athena* (1979). The municipality restored it 1999-2007 and reopened with Anthony Quinn's family present. The springs themselves have dried; this is now a swimming cove with pergolas.

Practical: open daily 08:00-20:00 (1 May-31 Oct), €5 adult (2026 official price). Sunbed pair €25-30 at the "Kallithea Springs Seaside" restaurant on the water; bring a towel and swim shoes (rocky entry). Arrive before 10:00 for calm and light. Bus "Kalithea Springs" stop (line to Faliraki, €2.50, ~20 min, roughly every 20 min) is a few minutes' walk from the gate. D&D read: an abandoned sanatorium-temple of a healing god, re-consecrated - the mosaics are wards, the dry spring a quest hook.

## Option B - the Grande Albergo delle Rose and the casino
On the north beach, the hotel Governor Lago built to put Rhodes on the map: designed by Florestano Di Fausto with Michele Platania, built from 26 March 1925, opened 24 May 1927, "Byzantine, Arabian, Ottoman and Venetian lines", the "Dream of the East". A casino opened here in 1929. In 1936 the next governor, Cesare Maria De Vecchi, stripped its Deco ornament for a plainer "rurale" line - what you see today. Fact, not legend: in early 1949 UN mediator Ralph Bunche made Rhodes his headquarters and, in this hotel, brokered the Israel-Egypt armistice signed on 24 February 1949 (Lebanon, Jordan and Syria followed). Bunche had a local potter make commemorative plates for each negotiator; when Moshe Dayan asked what would have happened without an agreement, Bunche said, "I'd have broken the plates over your damn heads." He won the 1950 Nobel Peace Prize. Churchill, Venizelos, Onassis and Karamanlis all signed the register. The hotel closed in the mid-1970s, reopened as Casino Rodos on 2 April 1999 and was renovated in 2002 with 33 suites named for Greek gods.

For a non-gambler: the Grand Beach Restaurant (deck on the beach in front of the hotel, +30 22410 97437), the Aqua Seaside Cafe and Grand Asia (pan-Asian, in the casino garden, +30 22410 57700) are outward-facing venues; the Grand View Cocktail Bar sits inside the casino. Casino entry: 21+, passport or ID, summer "casual" code (jeans, shorts, sneakers OK; no swimwear, flip-flops, gym shorts). Slots run all day; live tables on a Wednesday start 17:00. Entry fee is reported variously as €6, €15 or free - verify at the door.

## Option C - the Foro Italico walk (Mandraki, 40 minutes)
Di Fausto's 1926 plan kept the medieval city intact and laid a new "garden city" along Mandraki, the harbour road renamed Foro Italico. He later claimed "no less than fifty buildings ... thirty two of them already built or in construction in 1927." Walk north from the Nea Agora: a seven-sided Moorish market (1920s) whose open courtyard holds an ornate domed pavilion, the old fish market - look for the fish reliefs. Next, the Bank of Greece (1930, Banca d'Italia; unsigned blueprints drawn in Rome, eleven versions in the archive, deliberately echoing the Knights' stonework). Then the Courthouse and Post Office, and the Governor's Palace (1926-27), Di Fausto's Doge's Palace in pink-and-white stone with a walkable arcade at street level. Across the square, the Evangelismos church (1924-25, Di Fausto and Rodolfo Petracco), a three-aisled Gothic basilica rebuilt from engravings of the Knights' cathedral of St John, which had stood beside the Grand Master's Palace until a lightning-struck powder magazine blew it up in 1856. Catholic until 1948, then Orthodox cathedral; Fotis Kontoglou frescoed the interior 1951/52-1961/62 in neo-Byzantine style, an Italian Gothic shell with Byzantine saints. Around Eleftherias Square stand the National Theatre (Teatro Puccini, 1937, 1,200 seats) and the Town Hall (Casa del Fascio, 1939). Finish at the harbour mouth: bronze stag Elafos and doe Elafina on their columns where legend, and no evidence, puts the Colossus's feet; Fort St Nicholas and three windmills on the mole. At the northern tip the Aquarium (1934-35, Bernabiti, Art Deco; open 09:00-20:00, last entry 19:20) is a basement "underwater cave" with a 2,000-year-old monk-seal skeleton from the port.

## Option D - water and sky
Rhodes Rent a Boat (Mandraki, +30 695 166 6454) runs a semi-private East Coast cruise, 4 h, €60, departing 09:45 (Afantou caves, Anthony Quinn Bay, Kallithea) - tight but feasible; private skippered sailing is 2 h from €260, 3 h from €360 (up to 10 guests), a birthday-sized splurge. Alternatively taxi to Anthony Quinn Bay: emerald, pine-fringed, the island's best snorkelling, sunbeds ~€15 each; late September is the "perfect balance". Two stories circulate about the name - that Quinn bought the cove during *Navarone*, or that Greece gifted it - treat both as legend. Nearer, Elli Beach is 10 minutes from Mandraki: Elli World (Pl. Kountourioti 6, May-Oct, cocktails and a Mediterranean menu) and Ronda next door; sunbed sets €12-30, front row far more in peak season.

## Special tickets and small luxuries
Palace of the Grand Master: €20, daily 08:00-20:00 (1 Apr-31 Oct, official); on Wednesdays the "Ancient Rhodes - 2,400 years" exhibition is open 09:00-17:00. Walls walk: buy the €5 (2025; €6 quoted 2026) ticket at the palace desk, then find the unmarked iron gate on the left of the forecourt - "no signage, just a custodian who inspects your ticket" - Monday-Friday 12:00-15:00, from the Gate of St Anthony round to the sea. Hammam: the Yeni Hammam (1558) on Arionos Square is closed to bathing; for a massage, Rodos Park Suites & Spa (Riga Fereou 12) is the historic-quarter option, day access unverified. Coffee: Monk / The Pure Roasters (Ethnarchou Makariou 14, 07:30-19:00), or Unik on Amerikis. Cake: Stani (since 1952, Agias Anastasias) for the island's classic ice cream and baklava; Savazio (Agias Anastasias 125) for artisanal tarts.
