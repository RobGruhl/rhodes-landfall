# Rhodian specialties and an 'edible quest' list for Rhodes town (pitaroudia, melekouni, souma, honey, lakani, giaprakia, kapamas, koulouria, moschopougia, sweets, the Nea Agora, Wednesday market, wines) — for Wed 30 Sep 2026

# The Edible Quest of Rhodes — script notes

## The one thing to know first
The Nea Agora (New Market), the heptagonal Italian market-fortress that every food guide sends you to, is a **closed construction site**. All shops shut on 17 November 2025 for a full restoration; 52 interior shops were demolished, services are being re-laid, and in June 2026 the shopkeepers' president said tenants hoped to re-enter "by October" with "most shops open by Christmas" and a full restart for the 2027 season. On 30 September 2026 the traveler should expect scaffolding, restored facades and a locked or half-open courtyard, not a market. Treat it as a monument to look at from Mandraki, and move the eating elsewhere. Script it as the sealed dungeon: "the doors are barred; the vault is being re-warded."

## The monument itself (for the walk past)
Governor Mario Lago decided in 1923 to move all trade outside the medieval walls; architect Florestano Di Fausto drew the New Market and its Fish Market in Rome in 1924 and the complex opened in 1926 (it is often labelled "Mercato Nuovo 1927"). It is a seven-sided ring of shops with domes and a monumental Gothic-arched gate on 7 Martiou (Evdomis Martiou) avenue facing Mandraki. In the courtyard stands the hexagonal Fish Market pavilion, its dome pierced with porthole windows and its arcade columns carrying "oriental" detailing — the last place fish was gutted and sold under Italian rule. An office floor was added in the 1960s. Read it as a bazaar-fortress with a domed shrine at its heart: the shrine is the ichthyagora, the fishmongers' rotunda.

## The quest items (what is truly Rhodian)
**Pitaroudia — common item, first to find.** Chickpea fritters, the "national dish of Rhodes": ground soaked chickpeas, onion, grated tomato, spearmint, cumin, flour, shallow-fried into big plump patties (not the small revithokeftedes of other islands). On Nisyros the same idea uses yellow split peas and oregano. Order them as a meze with souma or beer.

**Melekouni — rare item, the birthday relic.** A diamond-shaped bar of at least 40% honey and 40% unhulled toasted sesame, with toasted almonds, cinnamon/nutmeg/clove/coriander and citrus zest; soft and pliable, never brittle like pasteli, because only honey binds it. Registered PGI (EU file PGI-GR-02208; published 2017, in force from 2018). The name is Rhodian dialect: meli (honey) + kounni (seed). The EU dossier quotes folklorist Konstantinidis: it is "the official treat at village weddings and festivals and sent as a wedding announcement to friends and relatives unable to attend, like sugared almonds in the towns." In Maritsa the bride's and groom's parents went door to door calling "come and make melekouni!"; men with musicians left a melekouni in every house as the invitation. Rhodes made the Guinness "longest melekouni" on 26 September 2010 and the island's barter network uses melekouni as its unit of exchange. The wedding wish — "may you live as many years as there are sesame seeds in the piece" — converts directly into a birthday blessing. Buy it in delis on Sokratous street or from Melissokomiki Dodekanisou (Bee Museum shop, Pastida).

**Souma — the potion.** Clear pomace spirit, about 50% ABV, distilled in copper kazania in Embonas and Siana; the pomace ferments about a month, the "head" is strong, the "tail" goes back in the next batch. Visit Greece's line: "the process begins in late September, immediately after the harvest" — the traveler arrives on the very days the stills are lit. Add water and it turns milky, like ouzo, but the aroma is grape not anise. Locals: "drinking souma is drinking the nectar of Rhodes." Serve with cheese, olives, rusks, seafood.

**Rhodian thyme honey.** Melissokomiki Dodekanisou (founded by Nikos Melissourgos and Nikos Retsas; Bee Museum at 5th km Tsairi–Airport, Pastida) bottles a thyme honey with ten international taste awards; it is the base of proper melekouni.

**Lakani — legendary item (probably uncompletable in port).** Named for the deep clay lakani pot. Goat (or lamb) with chickpeas, coarse bulgur or trahanas, onion, tomato, cumin and cinnamon, sealed in a wood oven overnight — traditionally the oven door was plastered shut with mud and opened next day. Home village Archangelos, made for weddings, panigyria and the feast of Sts Peter and Paul (cooked 29 June, eaten 30 June). Lindos makes it without chickpeas; Siana with bulgur only. Some local-cuisine restaurants list it, but expect village tavernas rather than the Old Town; treat it as the quest that sends you back to the island.

**Giaprakia.** Rhodian dolmades (Turkish yaprak, leaf): a raw, unsautéed filling of goat or lamb mince, rice (historically bulgur), tomato, onion, dill, parsley, and the signature "distant fragrance" — cumin. The same filling stuffs zucchini flowers, cooked in the same pot, served room-temperature with yogurt.

**Kapamas (Lambriotis).** The Easter roast of the villages: a whole kid or lamb stuffed with rice, its own organs, onion, tomato, raisins, pine nuts, cumin, cinnamon and herbs, baked overnight from Holy Saturday. Out of season in September; mention it, do not promise it.

**Koulouria of Archangelos.** Not bread rings here: wedding pasta of whole-wheat flour, oil, water and salt, boiled in broth from goat bones, pork or veal, finished with butter and kefalotyri.

**Moschopougia.** Half-moon pastries of ground almond with sugar, cinnamon, clove and nutmeg, dipped in rosewater and snowed with icing sugar; a bride carries a basket of them to her mother-in-law at engagement, on feast days and May Day. Takakia (Archangelos) are the honey-syruped nut rolls; koufalaka the local loukoumades-like fritters.

**Breads and cheese.** Rhodian spice breads leavened with mastic and heavy with sesame; eliopita and little eliotes rolls with olive, onion and mint; cumin in doughs and paximadia. Cheeses are goat cheese, feta, kefalotyri and especially mizithra, which fills the wild-greens pougia. Halva and galaktoboureko are pan-Greek, not Rhodian — order the milk pie at Stani (founded 1931) for the birthday rather than claim it as local lore.

## Where to actually get it on a Wednesday
- **Ippokratous Square to Sokratous street** is the shopping lane of the Old Town; delis here sell melekouni, thyme honey, souma and olive oil (open roughly 09:00–20:00+).
- **Fournariko**, Sokratous 41 at Platonos/Dinokratous, wood-oven bakery using flour from Eleousa village, open 24 hours, spanakopita and orange cake praised.
- **Old Town Corner Bakery**, Omirou 88, for croissants, baklava, orange cake and what reviewers call the best coffee in Rhodes.
- **Koukos**, Mandilara 20-22 (New Town, 1930s listed house), wood-fired bread, pies and a cellar of local wine.
- **Laiki Agora of Agios Dimitrios**, Klavdiou Pepper street by the Taxiarches cemetery and Zefyros beach: Wednesday and Saturday about 08:00–14:00 — island produce, honey, capers, vine leaves, cheese. 30 September is a Wednesday, so this replaces the closed Nea Agora as the market stop.
- **Vintage Wine Store**, Alexandrou Diakou (New Town), for CAIR and Emery bottles; **Cellar of the Knights** near Sokratous is recommended by forum users but its street is unverified.
- **Lukumamma** (Amarantou 24) is the loukoumades temple but opens at 18:00 — after all-aboard. Note it as "the shop you cannot reach today."

## Wines to carry home
Two PDOs: Rhodos (white Athiri, red Mandilaria/Amorgiano) and Muscat of Rhodes. CAIR, founded 1928 as Compagnia Agricola Industriale Rodi by four Italians and two Italian-Jewish partners, passed to Greek cooperative control in 1954 and made Greece's first traditional-method sparkling wine; its winery is at the 2nd km of Lindou avenue. Emery (Triantafyllou family, distillers since 1923, tasting room in Embonas 11:00–17:00) makes Villaré, a 100% Athiri white. Smaller names: Alexandris (Embonas), Kounaki (1928, Embonas), Piperis (Apollona), Korkidas (Salakos). A CAIR Brut or Emery Villaré is the birthday bottle.
