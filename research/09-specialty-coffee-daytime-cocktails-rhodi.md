# Specialty coffee, daytime cocktails, Rhodian wine and souma in Rhodes town (Old Town, Mandraki, New Town) for Wednesday 30 September 2026, 09:00-17:30

## Coffee, wine and souma in Rhodes town: a scriptwriter's brief

**The lay of the land.** The ship ties up at the Tourist Port on Akti Sachtouri, at the eastern foot of the walls. Everything in this brief is inside a 2 km radius: the medieval Old Town directly west of the quay; Mandraki, the small harbour with the windmills and the deer columns, ten minutes north; and the Italian-built New Town (Niochori) that wraps around Mandraki and runs up to Elli beach at the island's northern tip. Nothing here needs a taxi.

**How Greeks drink coffee.** The island runs on cold espresso. Order a *freddo espresso* (double shot shaken over ice, sugar to taste: *sketo* none, *metrio* medium, *glyko* sweet) or a *freddo cappuccino* (the same with cold-foamed milk). A *frappé* is the older instant-coffee version; *ellinikos* is the thick boiled coffee in a small cup. Town prices run about EUR 2 to 4.50 a drink and EUR 1.50 to 3 for a pastry (looneytravels.com), though the tourist-facing Old Town and the beach clubs charge much more.

**The third-wave stop: Monk Coffee / The Pure Roasters (New Town, 08:00 opening).** This is the island's serious coffee address, at Ethnarchou Makariou 14, a few minutes' walk uphill from the Mandraki windmills. Independent reviewers call the Monk / Ara:zo team "the only ones who do third-wave coffee" on Rhodes (dripsofgod.com); the beans come from Samba Coffee in Athens with three single origins on offer, and the shop also sells bags. Order the freddo cappuccino with oat milk, a flat white, or an Ethiopian single-origin filter; a cappuccino is about EUR 3 to 3.20 (coffeeradar.io). Hours: Monday to Saturday from 08:00 (closing listed as 19:00 by one directory and 21:00 by Tripadvisor); closed Sunday, so Wednesday is fine. Its sister cafe Ara:zo (Agiou Ioanni 156, 08:00-18:00) is the brunch venue with the same coffee. Two blocks away, **Unik Specialty Coffee Brewers** (Amerikis 42, Mon-Sat 07:30-19:30) is run by two brothers; its cold brew is described as "one of the cleanest-tasting in Rhodes" and there is homemade halva. Note: I could not find any venue called "Bible Coffee" in Rhodes in any directory, map database or review site; see open questions.

**The story stop: Koukos (Mandilara 20-22, from 08:00).** Koukos is a rambling Rhodian guesthouse-cafe on the pedestrian street next to the National Theatre (itself a 1937 Bernabiti building, formerly the Teatro Puccini). Founded in 1999 according to a local blog, it is decorated floor to ceiling with old objects, and the pies come out of a wood oven each morning; the little madeleine-like cakes arrive free with your coffee. It is the place to sit at a marble table with a Greek coffee and a cheese pie and let the audio talk about the Knights.

**The birthday sweet: Stani (Agias Anastasias 28, daily 07:30-24:00).** "Stani" means sheepfold. The story told locally is that Ibrahim Sarri Hasan, arriving from Turkey, opened a dairy shop in 1952 selling milk and yogurt, and later became famous for his ice-cream recipe (search summary of foodaroundgreece.gr; the page itself is behind a bot wall, so treat as medium confidence). Today it is a bakery-patisserie chain and the classic branch sits just south of the walls. Order the ice cream: stracciatella and mastic with wild cherry are singled out (Tripadvisor, July 2026), plus the milk pie described as "light, creamy, soft, milky." Reviewers repeatedly praise the product and grumble about brusque service; enjoy the ice cream on the walk back to the St John's Gate.

**The harbour bar: Swan by Kontiki (Mandraki quay, daily 10:00-01:00).** Kontiki was for decades a floating cafe-restaurant moored in Mandraki with floor-to-ceiling harbour windows and live music upstairs (iliosartblog). Its successor, Swan, is a three-level floating venue moored off Plateia Neoriou by the New Market: a 10:00-13:00 "Morning Ritual" of coffee and brunch, all-day dining and sushi, cocktails on the top deck, and "360 degree view of sea and city." It is expensive (starters about EUR 26.50, mains EUR 37-52, oysters EUR 9 each) and 2026 reviews are split on service, but for a birthday cocktail at 15:00 with the windmills, the fort of St Nicholas and the walls in view, it is the most theatrical seat in town. Sunset on 30 September is roughly 19:00, after the ship sails, so plan the harbour drink for the golden mid-afternoon rather than dusk.

**The Grand Hotel of Roses (Papanikolaou 4).** The Italian governor Mario Lago commissioned a luxury hotel in a style mixing "Byzantine, Arab, Ottoman and Venetian lines" (casinorodos.gr); it was designed by Florestano Di Fausto with Michele Platania and opened on 24 May 1927. Its Art Deco flourishes were "cleansed" in the late 1930s under the next governor, De Vecchi (Wikipedia, Di Fausto). It reopened as Casino Rodos in 1999 and now houses a 33-suite boutique hotel. The ground-floor Andromeda Lounge & Bar is listed at 11:00-03:00 year-round (finesthotels.net; not re-verified), while the rooftop Grand View Cocktail Bar is reached through the casino, which requires 21+ ID registration. A martini in the 1927 lobby is the "classy" birthday drink; whether non-residents are welcome in the lounge should be confirmed on the day.

**Elli beach, the 1930s lido.** The white complex at the top of the beach is the Elli bathing establishment, built 1936-38 by Armando Bernabiti with a "circular hall, arched doors, and vaulted ceiling," boats below and changing rooms above, protected since 1987 (yachtcharterfleet.com; Wikipedia, Bernabiti). It now houses Ronda (daily 07:30-24:00) and Elli World (08:00-01:00): loungers, frozen coffees, Hugo and Margarita cocktails, and a view across the strait to the mountains of Turkey. Reviews from summer 2026 are savage about prices and service ("EUR 16 for two slices of bread"); go for a single drink and the diving platform, not lunch.

**Old Town rooftops and gardens.** The Minos Pension roof garden (Omirou 5, May-October, 08:00-23:00, "everybody is welcome") is at the highest point inside the walls, opposite an old windmill; it is the Old Town's best quiet afternoon-drink viewpoint, with the domes, the walls and the sea in one sweep. Christos Garden (Griva 102 in the New Town, since 1980) is a pebble-mosaic courtyard bar run by a mother and son, cocktails from about EUR 13, but hours conflict between sources (08:00-24:00 vs 18:00-02:00); call ahead. Macao (Archelaou 5, cocktails EUR 17-18) opens only at 22:00, so it is off the table for a day visit. Socratous Garden (Sokratous 124, 09:00-23:00) is a fountain courtyard for a break from the bazaar street.

**Wine under the Kastellania.** Plateia Ippokratous is the heart of the lower town, and its landmark is the Kastellania, built 1507 with its external stone staircase; German Wikipedia calls it the Order's court of justice, local sources the Knights' commercial court (both are plausible; a courthouse either way). Under those stairs, since 1963, a family has run the Cellar of Knights (Wed 11:00-23:00): a bottle shop that pours "excellent Greek wines by glass" on the square's steps, keeps bottles open for sampling and stocks Rhodian labels at near-supermarket prices. Two hotel wine bars deepen the theme: The Wine Bar at The Wine Hotel (Platonos 41; Wed 07:00-23:30 per Google, tastings by booking) pours CAIR and Emery labels such as Ilios, Grand Maitre and Chevalier de Rhodes, and 10GR (Polidorou 16, from 13:00) lists 250+ Greek labels with a "Rhodian Wine Tasting" flight.

**What is in the glass.** Rhodes exported wine by the 7th century BC; stamped amphora handles have turned up across the Mediterranean (dianekochilas.com). PDO Rhodes whites must be at least 70% Athiri (with Assyrtiko, Malagousia), reds and roses at least 70% Amorgiano, the local name for Mandilaria (winesofgreece.org). Athiri gives "a gentle aroma and a fresh fruity taste"; Amorgiano gives reds "with good body and a spicy lively aroma." A separate PDO Muscat of Rhodes (1971) covers sweet wines from Muscat White and Muscat di Trani, a clone the Italians brought from Apulia during their rule (1912-47). CAIR, founded in 1928 by the Italians on the Lindos road, is Greece's leading bottle-fermented sparkling producer; Emery in Embonas began as distillers in 1923, moved into wine in 1966 and built its winery in 1974; Kounaki (1928) runs 11:00-19:00 tastings from EUR 6 for seven wines to EUR 20 for fourteen.

**Souma.** The island's spirit is a grape-pomace distillate from the mountain villages of Embonas and Siana, boiled in copper stills after the late-September harvest, which means the new batch is being made the very week of this visit. It is "particularly strong and pure," turns milky white with water or ice, and is drunk with cheese, olives, rusks and seafood (visitgreece.gr); typical strength is 38-45% but Rhodian souma "may exceed 60%" (gastronomytours.com). Siana has "the only legal souma distillery in the Dodecanese" (rhodesislander.com). In town, ask for it at the Cellar of Knights, Koukos or any meze taverna; it is poured in shot glasses over a couple of ice cubes.

**D&D reading.** The Kastellania is the Order's courthouse and the tavern beneath its stairs is where the party buys rumours; souma is the mountain potion that halves your Wisdom save; the Grand Hotel of Roses is a colonial governor's pleasure palace turned gambling den, its Art Deco ornament scoured off by a hard-line successor; and the floating Swan is the ship-tavern anchored where, legend says, the Colossus once stood astride the harbour mouth (it did not; that part is legend).
